# Findings

Function-word stylometry over the Greek text of 39 works, 830,726 tokens.
Every number below is regenerated by `python3 run_all.py`; the JSON is in
`results/`.

Read the caveats at the bottom before quoting any of it. Two of the four
studies produced a clear result, one produced a useful negative, and one
produced a result that dissolved under a length correction.

---

## 1. The pseudo-Aristotelian works separate from the genuine ones

Each work was scored by Burrows's Delta from the centroid of the genuine works,
leaving the work itself out of that centroid. The authorship labels come from
the standard handbooks and were used **only to grade the result afterwards** —
the clustering never saw them.

| | n | mean Δ to core |
|---|---|---|
| genuine | 25 | 0.673 |
| consensus spurious | 10 | 0.753 |

Difference +0.081, permutation *p* = 0.0013, Cliff's *d* = +0.65,
**AUC = 0.824**. Of the ten most distant works, six are the consensus spuria
(chance ≈ 2.8).

The errors are the interesting part, and they are systematic rather than
random. The genuine works wrongly ranked as distant are **Topics, Prior
Analytics, Posterior Analytics and Physics** — the four most formal works in
the corpus. PC1 is carried by εἰ, ὅτι, τι, οὐκ, τῶν: this axis is *logical
formalism vs. discursive prose*, not authorship.

So the headline AUC is partly measuring genre. Scoring each spurious work only
against genuine works of comparable kind and length fixes this:

| genre-matched comparison | genuine mean | spurious mean | AUC |
|---|---|---|---|
| short natural treatises (7 genuine vs 9 spurious) | 0.621 | 0.752 | **0.921** |
| practical philosophy (EN, EE, Pol, Rhet vs Oeconomica) | 0.691 | 0.836 | **1.000** |

Within genre the signal is much stronger than across it. `De Melisso
Xenophane Gorgia` is the most stylistically alien work in the entire corpus
(Δ = 0.893 to the core, 0.956 genre-matched); `De Spiritu`, `Mechanica`, `On
Colours` and `On Indivisible Lines` sit inside the genuine range and are not
detected.

## 2. The Ethics common books side with the *Eudemian* — the headline result

Nicomachean Ethics V–VII and Eudemian Ethics IV–VI are the same text. Which
treatise it was written for has been argued since the 19th century; Anthony
Kenny (*The Aristotelian Ethics*, 1978) argued on particle-frequency grounds
that it belongs to the Eudemian, against the traditional Nicomachean ascription.

This corpus makes the question unusually clean to test. Because every modern
editor prints the common books inside the Nicomachean Ethics, they are in the
**same file, by the same editor** (Bywater's 1894 OCT) as the rest of the EN. So
the comparison "common books vs. rest of the EN" has no edition confound at all.

**Every one of the ten surviving EN books was classified, held out one at a
time, against the EN and EE centroids:**

| book | Δ to EN-proper | Δ to EE | nearer |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | 0.665 | 0.722 | EN |
| II | 0.629 | 0.686 | EN |
| III | 0.519 | 0.665 | EN |
| IV | 0.538 | 0.660 | EN |
| **V** | **0.674** | **0.535** | **EE** |
| **VI** | **0.679** | **0.618** | **EE** |
| **VII** | **0.598** | **0.511** | **EE** |
| VIII | 0.570 | 0.793 | EN |
| IX | 0.520 | 0.708 | EN |
| X | 0.472 | 0.630 | EN |

Ten out of ten agree with the philological tradition about which three books are
the common books — and all three go to the Eudemian. Under the null that the
three EE-preferring books are an arbitrary three of ten, *p* = 1/C(10,3) =
**0.0083**.

That the seven undisputed books classify correctly is what makes this
interpretable: the method is not simply pulling everything toward EE.

At chunk level (2,000-token samples): Δ(common books → EN centroid) = 0.700 vs
Δ(EN-proper → EN centroid) = 0.608, permutation *p* = 0.0003, Cliff's *d* =
+0.81, and **8 of 8 common-book chunks are nearer the Eudemian centroid.**

### It survives everything I tried

| test | result |
|---|---|
| chunk size 1000 / 1500 / 2000 / 2500 / 3000 | EE preferred at every size (16/17, 11/11, 8/8, 7/7, 6/6) |
| 200 random half-vocabularies | EE nearer in **200/200** |
| connectives only / article only / prepositions only / no article+pronouns | EE preferred in all |
| particles only (14 features) | 4/8 — too few features to decide |
| ablating the top discriminators | degrades, but this selects features on the tested contrast and is circular; F is the unbiased version |

### Why the edition confound does not explain it

The obvious objection is that EE here is Susemihl's 1884 Teubner while EN is
Bywater's 1894 OCT, so the result could be measuring editors. Three things
answer that:

1. **The structural point.** The common books and the rest of the EN come from
   the same editor and the same edition. Whatever Bywater did, he did to both.
   The difference between them cannot be Bywater-vs-Susemihl.
2. **The Ross control.** W. D. Ross edited four works spanning four genres
   (Physics, Metaphysics, Politics, Rhetoric). If the editor drove the
   clustering they would clump together. They do not — mean Δ between two
   Ross-edited works is **0.981**, versus **0.939** from Ross-edited to
   other-edited. Editor is not the organising signal.
3. **Elision and movable nu are not editorial in this corpus.** Editor explains
   η² = 0.036 of the variance in elision rate; within Bekker's 1837 text alone
   it runs from 0.73% to 4.15%. These features track the work, not the hand that
   printed it.

Point 3 was a **failed prediction**, and it killed the dissociation test this
analysis was designed around. I expected the common books to carry Bywater's
printing conventions while their particle usage tracked the Eudemian, which
would have separated the two layers cleanly. Instead the common books side with
the EE on elision (4.17% vs EN-proper's 4.42%) and on movable nu (50.6% vs
56.8%) as well. That is not the confound returning — these are not editorial
features — but it is not independent evidence either.

**The alternative explanation that survives:** if Bywater took his text of books
V–VII from a different manuscript tradition, or leaned on Susemihl when printing
them, the whole pattern would follow without any authorial difference. Settling
that needs the apparatus criticus, not a token count.

### Which words carry it

More frequent in the common books: ἀλλά, μόνον, ὥστε, ὅταν, μέν, οὐκ.
Less frequent: ἴσως, δή, μάλιστα, μᾶλλον, ἄν, ἑκάστῳ, τοῖς, πᾶσι.

## 3. Seams inside single works — one strong hit, and one result that dissolved

Each book was scored against the centroid of the *other books of its own
treatise*, which holds work, genre, editor and manuscript tradition constant
simultaneously. This is the best-controlled comparison here.

**A length correction is essential and changes the answer.** Across the 72
books, correlation between log length and raw Delta is **−0.487**: shorter books
drift from any centroid for purely statistical reasons. Ranking by raw Delta
mostly ranks by shortness. Books are therefore ranked by the residual from a
regression of Delta on log length.

Most distinctive books in the corpus, length-corrected:

| | residual | status in the literature |
|---|---|---|
| **History of Animals X** | **+0.444** | widely rejected as post-Aristotelian |
| **Parts of Animals I** | **+0.434** | methodological introduction, often treated as separate |
| Generation of Animals I | +0.226 | — |
| Generation of Animals V | +0.201 | — |
| Politics I | +0.191 | — |

HA X is the single most extreme book in the corpus and is the one book here
that scholarship rejects outright (AUC 1.00 against the other nine HA books).
PA I, the general preface to the biological works, comes second.

**What dissolved.** On raw Delta, Metaphysics α *elatton* — ascribed to Pasicles
of Rhodes already in antiquity — is the biggest outlier in the Metaphysics
(Δ = 1.196, z = +2.69), which looks like a spectacular confirmation. It is also
the shortest book in the treatise at 1,149 tokens. After the length correction
its residual is +0.104, still first within the Metaphysics but unremarkable on
the corpus scale, well below HA X and PA I. **The apparent confirmation was
mostly an artefact of its length.**

**Clear negatives, worth recording.** Metaphysics Λ, often held to be an
independent lecture, is the *least* distinctive book in the treatise
(z = −1.33). Metaphysics K, whose authenticity has long been doubted, is
unremarkable (z = −0.04). Politics VII, central to Jaeger's developmental
argument about book order, is second-*least* distinctive (z = −1.37). Physics
VII, which survives in two recensions, does not stand out (z = −0.22). On this
measure those four traditional seams are invisible.

The Nicomachean common books show up again here independently: AUC 0.86 against
the other EN books.

## Caveats

- **No out-of-corpus author control.** Theophrastus or Plato would calibrate how
  large a between-author Delta ought to be; nothing external could be fetched
  from this environment. The spuria are the only "different author" signal, and
  they are many different authors, not one.
- **Missing works.** Categories, De Interpretatione and the Isagoge have no
  Greek TEI here (they need the local TLG disc). Magna Moralia is absent
  entirely, which matters for the Ethics question specifically — it is the third
  ethical treatise and Kenny's argument uses it.
- **Genre is not fully controlled** in Study 1, as the study itself shows.
- **Chunks within a work are contiguous** and therefore not independent samples,
  so chunk-level *p*-values are optimistic. The book-level exact test
  (*p* = 0.0083) does not have this problem and is the one to quote.
- **Editions differ across the corpus.** The Ross control argues this is not
  driving the clustering, and the common-books comparison is edition-controlled
  by construction, but no such control is perfect.
- **Stylometry does not date or attribute anything on its own.** "These books
  group with the Eudemian" is a fact about particle frequencies. Whether that
  means Aristotle wrote them for the Eudemian course, or revised them later, or
  a pupil transmitted them, is not something a token count can settle.
