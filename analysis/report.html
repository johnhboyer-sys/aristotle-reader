Why count the small words

Content words tell you what a text is about. In a corpus that runs from the dissection of cuttlefish to the definition of justice, that is exactly the wrong signal — cluster on content and you recover the table of contents, not the author.

Function words are the opposite. μέν, δέ, γάρ, ἀλλά, ὥστε — particles, connectives, prepositions, the article — are semantically near-empty, indeclinable or nearly so, and enormously frequent. Their rates track how a writer builds a sentence rather than what the sentence says. They are also, usefully, the words an author has no reason to think about. Here they account for 53.3% of every token in the corpus.

This is the class Anthony Kenny counted by hand in The Aristotelian Ethics (1978), for the same reasons. What follows re-runs that kind of question over the whole corpus at once.

The common books side with the Eudemian

Nicomachean Ethics V–VII and Eudemian Ethics IV–VI are not similar texts. They are the same text. Every modern editor prints it once, inside the Nicomachean Ethics, and cross-references it from the Eudemian. Which treatise it was written for is the oldest live question in Aristotelian scholarship.

That printing convention is what makes this testable cleanly. Because the common books sit inside the Nicomachean Ethics, they are in the same file, set by the same editor — Bywater's 1894 Oxford text — as the books they are being compared against. No editorial difference can separate them.

The result in one number

Ten out of ten books agree with the philological tradition about which three are the common books — and all three go to the Eudemian. Under the null that the three Eudemian-leaning books are an arbitrary three of ten, p = 1/C(10,3) = 0.0083.

That the seven undisputed books classify correctly is what makes the other three interpretable. The method is not simply dragging everything toward the Eudemian.

The objection that survives

Not the editions. W. D. Ross edited four works across four genres; if the editor drove the clustering they would clump, and they do not — mean Δ between two Ross-edited works is 0.981, versus 0.939 from Ross to everyone else.

What a token count cannot settle is the manuscript tradition. If Bywater took his text of books V–VII from a different family, or leaned on Susemihl's Eudemian when printing them, this entire pattern would follow with no authorial difference at all. That question belongs to the apparatus criticus.

The corpus knows which works are not Aristotle's

The Corpus Aristotelicum carries about a dozen works that essentially nobody now attributes to Aristotle. That consensus was reached philologically, over four centuries, without counting anything — which makes it an answer key written in advance by someone else. The labels were used only to grade the result; the clustering never saw them.

The errors are the interesting part, because they are systematic rather than random. The genuine works wrongly ranked as distant are the Topics, both Analytics and the Physics — the four most formal works in the corpus. The first principal component is carried by εἰ, ὅτι, τι, οὐκ: that axis is logical formalism against discursive prose, not authorship.

One book stands out from its own treatise

Aristotle's treatises are not books in the modern sense; they are lecture material assembled after his death, and particular books have long been argued to sit oddly inside particular treatises. Scoring each book against the other books of its own treatise holds work, genre, editor and manuscript tradition constant at once — the best-controlled comparison available here.

History of Animals X is the most extreme book in the corpus, and it is the one book here that scholarship rejects outright — it scores AUC 1.00 against the other nine books of its treatise. Parts of Animals I, the general methodological preface to the biological works, comes second.

What did not work

Three results went the wrong way, and they matter as much as the ones that went right.

  • Metaphysics α elatton dissolved. On raw Δ it is the biggest outlier in the Metaphysics — which looks like a spectacular confirmation of the ancient ascription to Pasicles of Rhodes. It is also the shortest book in the treatise, at 1,149 tokens. After the length correction its residual is unremarkable. The confirmation was mostly an artefact of its length, and I would have reported it if I had not checked.
  • A designed control failed outright. The plan was to show that elision and movable nu track the modern editor while particles track the author, separating the two layers cleanly. They do not: editor explains η² = 0.036 of the variance in elision rate, and within Bekker's 1837 text alone it runs from 0.73% to 4.15%. These are features of the transmitted work, not of the printing house. The test was abandoned, not reinterpreted.
  • Famous seams are invisible. Metaphysics Λ, often held to be an independent lecture, is the least distinctive book in its treatise. Metaphysics K, whose authenticity has been doubted for a century, is unremarkable. Politics VII, central to Jaeger's developmental argument, is second-least distinctive. On this measure, four traditional seams simply are not there.

What this cannot tell you

  • There is no out-of-corpus author. Theophrastus or Plato would calibrate how large a between-author distance ought to be. The pseudo-Aristotelian works are the only different-author signal available, and they are many different authors rather than one control.
  • Three works are missing. Categories, De Interpretatione and the Isagoge have no Greek text in this repository. The Magna Moralia is absent entirely — a real loss here, since it is the third ethical treatise and Kenny's argument uses it.
  • Genre is not fully controlled in the authenticity study, as the study itself demonstrates.
  • Stylometry attributes nothing on its own. "These books group with the Eudemian" is a fact about particle frequencies. Whether that means Aristotle wrote them for the Eudemian course, revised them later, or a pupil transmitted them is not something a token count can decide.
