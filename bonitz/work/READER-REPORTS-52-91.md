# HYPOTHESES — TESTED 2026-08-06 (one confirmed, one refuted)

Two columns now show the same shape: a citation split across a line break
with a **period printed before the break**.

| column | line | reads | comparator verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| p053-L | l.13 `…10. 327.` / l.14 `b4.` | O `.` G `` L `` | **Opus-only** — neither other reader saw it |
| p059-R | l.5 `Ζγδ1. 766.` / l.6 `b7.` | not yet compared | ? |

If the 58–62 compare also shows the p059-R period as Opus-only, that is not a
source misprint at all but a **systematic Opus reader artifact**: inserting a
sentence-final period at a line break where a Bekker citation is split. That
would be the Opus analogue of the Sonnet ȣ→υ defect — a reader-slot flaw
found by the comparator, not by the reader.

Cheap deterministic test once flags exist: find every reconciled line ending
in `\d+\.$` whose next line begins `[ab]\d`, and check whether Opus is the
lone witness to the period. Do NOT record either as a Bonitz misprint until
this is settled.


## RESULT — tested deterministically, no agents

**CONFIRMED — Opus reads `ὡς` as `υἱς`.** `υἱς` occurs exactly twice in all 96
Opus reads, and both times Genie AND LlamaParse independently read `ὡ`:

| | opus | genie | llama |
|---|---|---|---|
| p055-L l.28 | `υἱςȣ̓` | `ὡςοὐ` | `ὡςȣ̓` |
| p059-L l.47 | `υἱ` | `ὡ` | `ὡ` |

p059-L settles it on sense too: `Ὅμηρον ἀναλαβὼν ὁ Μαίων ὡς ἴδιον ἔτρεφεν`
— "raised him as his own". `υἱς` is not a Greek word. Rare (2 in 96 columns)
but wrong 100% of the time it appears, and the comparator caught both.
**Adjudicators must not ratify `υἱς`.** Review item A2 stays demoted.

**REFUTED — the period before a split Bekker citation is NOT an Opus
artifact.** Six instances across the corpus; Genie agrees with Opus on
p017-R and p059-R l.4, and is absent only on p020-L and p053-L. So the
period is usually really printed; Opus over-reports it rather than inventing
