Parma against Corpus Thomisticum

Every word of Aquinas's commentary as Corpus Thomisticum gives it (the Turin 1959 text, keyed by Busa), set against the Parma edition, vol. 20 (1866), read from archive.org's scan. Only Aquinas's words: Parma's footnotes, its two Latin texts of Aristotle and its lectio summaries are left out. ct Corpus Thomisticum, p Parma.

The two texts print different words in {{N_ALL}} places out of 90,700 words. Every other difference is spelling, an abbreviation, or the scanner's error.

Most of the {{N_ALL}} are one letter of an ending (esse/esset, qua/quae). Some change the sense: II, l. 11, n. 10, where Parma lacks non, and I, l. 5, n. 3, where its lemma reads Democritus for Diogenes.

How it was done

  1. Corpus Thomisticum's three book pages, split into its 873 numbered paragraphs.
  2. Parma: archive.org's OCR of vol. 20 with its word boxes. Only lines in the commentary's type size were kept. Parma sets Aristotle's two Latin versions and its footnotes in smaller type, and its headings and summaries in larger.
  3. Both texts cut down to letters and lined up word by word.
  4. A difference was called scanner noise when Parma's form is not a Latin word and lies close to Corpus's word. The word list is 13,183 forms from Corpus's text of seven of Aquinas's Aristotle commentaries.
  5. Every other difference was cut from the page image and read a second time by another OCR engine (Tesseract). Where the second read gave Corpus's word, the place was dropped.
  6. The 167 places left were read by eye from the crops. {{N_ALL}} hold. Each crop links to its page of the scan.

What this cannot see

  • A real reading that the scanner garbled into a near copy of Corpus's word would be dropped at step 4. A control of 80 such places, read again from the scan, found none.
  • Parma's worn c often prints like e, so neither OCR engine tells cum from eum. These were read by eye and by sense, and none is counted.
  • The readings by eye are Claude's, from the crops shown here; no second reader has checked them.
  • One scan, one OCR. The 1948 photo reprint of Parma on archive.org is lending only.
