The Aristotle Reader: four directions

Four new looks for the whole site. Each one is drawn on the opening of the Nicomachean Ethics with the site’s own Greek, its four translations and its real parses, and the main controls work: click a Greek word, change the translation, compare two, open the contents, jump to a Bekker number. Keys: 1 to 4 pick a direction, the arrows step through the pages, t switches to dark, p shows a phone.

The site now

  1. It looks like every other humanities site. Parchment ground, brown buttons, rounded boxes, a centred title with a small capitals label over it. Nothing in it says Aristotle, or says that this is the best Greek–English Aristotle on the web.
  2. The home page is all boxes of one weight. Three link boxes, a support box, then forty-one work boxes in a grid. Nothing leads, and the boxes take the room that titles need.
  3. Bekker’s numbers hold the whole corpus together, and the site hides them. Every passage of Aristotle has one address from 1a1 to 1462b19, but the home and work pages never show where a work sits, and “Go to Bekker line” is a small button.
  4. The reader puts three rows of controls over the text. Contents, the work menu, Support, Search, Lexicon, Settings, Help and the theme; then Go to, ten book buttons and thirteen chapter boxes; then the translation menu, the view switch and Print. Then a tip box over the text.
  5. Translations are hard to choose and harder to compare. The picker is a browser drop-down of full citations, and comparing two translations is a radio button inside Settings.
  6. The word sidebar is a stack of boxes. Three πᾶς boxes repeat “all, fem nom/voc sg” because LSJ has three entries for the word.
  7. Search shows every option at once. Two fields, six rows of radio buttons, a works filter, a combine switch and a paragraph of help, all before the first result.

The same in all four

  • The Greek and the English each keep their own Bekker numbers: the Greek its exact line numbers, the English its own ticks, upright when fixed, italic when estimated.
  • The English is set as large as the Greek, in the same ink.
  • Click a Greek word and its parse opens in a sidebar on the right; on a phone it comes up from the bottom. The parses are the site’s real ones for every word in Book I.
  • The LSJ entry stays Grammata’s. The site cannot restyle that module, so all four show it in one neutral setting (here a stand-in, since an artifact cannot load it).
  • Every work is on the home page, and every work has its own page.
  • Search in Greek and English, the lexicon, phrases, Bekker jumps, footnotes, compare, Greek / Both / English, text size and width, light and dark.
  • No rounded cards, gradients or drop shadows, apart from menus that float over the text. Titles, descriptions, citations and data come from the site; the few new lines of interface copy are drafts.

What the mockup leaves out

  • Only Book I of the Ethics opens in the reader. The other works’ pages open from every home page, built from the registry.
  • Search shows one query, ἀρετή in any form, with its real counts across the corpus; lines from Book I carry their Greek and English.
  • The lexicon shows ἀρετή.
  • Phrases, the search guide, the glossary, the desktop app, Support and Texts & Licences would follow whichever direction you choose.
  • Links to Phrases, Support and the other readers are placeholders here.
  • Print cannot run inside an artifact; the control is there, the dialog is not.
