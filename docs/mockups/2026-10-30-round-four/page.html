What changed after your notes

  • B: “This picture doesn’t work for the title layout.” Every B poster, on the home page and on each work page, runs across the page at the home page’s size, and the work’s details come below it.
  • B: “Different ones for mobile and desktop.” Each work’s photograph is cut twice by hand: wide for a desktop (2.4 to 1) and tall for a phone (4 to 5). Compare the panes on the Ethics, the Rhetoric and On Sophistical Refutations, where a tall photograph had to give a wide cut. A work can take a second photograph for either shape instead; on the site one <picture> holds both. The cuts come from the Getty’s full-size originals, so the soft photographs are sharp now. Your review of the photographs comes later, if B goes ahead.
  • All: “The alternating Greek then English simply doesn’t work here.” On a phone the reader sets Greek and English side by side, each with its own Bekker numbers: the Greek’s in the left margin, the English’s in the right. Compare puts the second translation under the first, beside the Greek.
  • All: “Have this feature on each option.” The menu marks the current page in the accent colour in every direction, in italic only in D, and work pages mark Works.
  • All: “Don’t we have this at the top of the page too?” A work page lists its translations once, in the facts at the top; each name opens that translation. The separate list and the repeat in the credits are gone.
  • D: “Don’t like this font at all.” D’s Greek is now Gentium Book Plus, A’s Greek face, everywhere in D. EB Garamond stays for English and for the logo you liked.
  • D: “Text seems too big?” D’s search results are smaller.
  • D: “Not sure about the photo being sticky.” The Aldine page scrolls with the text.
  • D: “Too much space for the photo here on mobile” and “a landscape crop that acts like a banner”. On a phone each Aldine page gives way to a banner cut from its top: the woodcut headpiece and the title.
  • Noted, not changed: A’s word blocks on the phone’s home page (“Don’t change yet”) and on work pages (“Not loving this on works pages”).

I took the notes on the Greek font and on text size to be about D’s lexicon and search pages, since you left them in the same minute as your note on D’s logo. If you meant B’s, say so.

The same in all three

  • Chapters lead: the work pages list every chapter, the reader opens each chapter with its number and title, and its running head names the chapter you are in. Bekker numbers stay in the margins.
  • Click a Greek word and its parse opens in a sidebar on the right; on a phone it comes up from the bottom.
  • The LSJ entry stays Grammata’s; here a stand-in, since an artifact cannot load the module.
  • Greek and English at one size, in one ink. Each keeps its own Bekker numbers.
  • Every work is on the home page, and every work has its own page.
  • Search, the lexicon, compare, Greek / Both / English, footnotes, text settings, Bekker jumps, light and dark.

What the mockup leaves out

  • Only Book I of the Ethics opens in the reader. Every other work’s page opens from the home pages.
  • Search shows one query, ἀρετή in any form; the lexicon shows ἀρετή.
  • Phrases, Support, Texts & Licences and the other readers are placeholders. Print cannot run inside an artifact, so its button does nothing here.
  • Interface copy and image captions are drafts.
