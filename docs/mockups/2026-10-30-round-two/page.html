The Aristotle Reader: round two

Four directions again, now with more to look at. A is the Plato-family design from round one, rebuilt around a Greek word for each work. B, C and D are new, and each takes its pictures from a different source: early photographs, old diagrams, and the first printed edition of Aristotle. Every page is drawn at desktop and phone width side by side, on the site’s own Greek, translations and parses. Keys: 1 to 4 pick a direction, the arrows step through the pages, t switches to dark, p switches between both widths, desktop and phone.

What changed after your notes

  • “Too utilitarian a design. I want some style.” Each direction now has a picture of its own and type with more character. Interface copy is shorter and the pages have more room.
  • “Chapter divisions are more important than Bekker divisions.” Chapters lead everywhere: the work pages list every chapter with its title, the reader opens each chapter with its number and title, search cites book and chapter first, and the reader’s running head shows the chapter you are in. Bekker numbers stay, but only in the margins: Greek line numbers every five lines, and each translation’s own ticks.
  • Too hung up on Bekker numbers as design. The corpus line, the work rulers and the Bekker hover readout are gone.
  • “Neat the first time. Gets old fast” and “These measure bars are gimmicky.” No timelines, no bars and no charts anywhere. Counts are numbers in text.
  • Mobile. Every page now shows desktop and phone together. Click a word in either and both open it.

Round one stays where it was, so you can still look at the B and D reader layouts: round one ↗. Comments left on that page are not sent to me, which is why they are answered here.

The same in all four

  • Click a Greek word and its parse opens in a sidebar on the right; on a phone it comes up from the bottom. The parses are the site’s real ones for every word in Book I.
  • The LSJ entry stays Grammata’s. The site cannot restyle that module, so all four show it in one neutral setting (here a stand-in, since an artifact cannot load it).
  • Greek and English at one size, in one ink. Each keeps its own Bekker numbers in the margin.
  • Every work is on the home page, and every work has its own page.
  • Search, the lexicon, compare, Greek / Both / English, footnotes, text settings, Bekker jumps, light and dark.
  • No rounded cards, gradients or drop shadows, apart from menus that float over the text.

What the mockup leaves out

  • Only Book I of the Ethics opens in the reader. Every other work’s page opens from the home pages.
  • Search shows one query, ἀρετή in any form; the lexicon shows ἀρετή.
  • Phrases, Support, Texts & Licences and the other readers are placeholders. Print cannot run inside an artifact, so its button does nothing here.
  • C draws diagrams for fourteen works; the others show where they sit in the division.
  • D shows Aldine pages for 36 works. The Rhetoric and Poetics are not in the Aldine set, and the mockup has no page for three short spurious works.
  • Interface copy and image captions are drafts.
