# E · Spectrum

Every work of Aristotle has its own colour, and the colours run in the order of the corpus, so the forty works on one shelf make a spectrum. Colour does the work of a binding: it says which work, division and book you are in, and never touches the text you read.

## Why it suits Aristotle

He sorted everything, and the corpus is his sorting made into a library. On a shelf its order shows at a glance: logic in reds, natural philosophy from orange through yellow to green, the Metaphysics in teal, ethics and politics in blues, rhetoric and poetics in violet. The doubted works run through the same colours again in grey, the colour drained out.

## The pages

- Home: the shelf. Forty spines, the title down each and the Greek at its foot, a divider naming each division. On a phone the books lie flat in a stack.
- A work: its cover, a flat field of its colour with the title as large as the field allows. Each book is a block of that colour, one step from the next. At the foot, its neighbours on the shelf.
- Reader: white paper, black type. Book I opens on a page of the cover's colour; after that the colour shows only at the edges: the band at the head, the book button, the chapter numbers, the word you click.
- Search: hits sit under bands of their work's colour.
- Lexicon: the word large, then the works it appears in as a short shelf.

## Type and colour

Libertinus, one family with full polytonic Greek: Serif for reading, Serif Display for titles, Sans for controls. Neutral white paper. Forty-one colours set in OKLCH so the steps are even, each with its own inks and title tone, checked for contrast. Dark mode lowers every colour a step.

## The risk

Forty colours can tip into a paint chart or a rainbow. The cure is restraint: ink colours, flat fields, no gradients, no colour on the text. Spine titles read sideways, as on a real shelf.
