## Rejected, and why

- Bronze compass, fragment (Met 17.230.62), for On Indivisible Lines: one leg only; it reads as a rod.
- Pair of bronze cymbals (Met 13.225.5a, b), for De Audibilibus: the photograph shows one; it reads as a dish.
- Gold necklace with crescent pendant (Met 21.88.90) and the Athenian owl with a crescent moon (Cleveland 1941.296.b), for the Posterior Analytics' eclipse: a crescent is not an eclipse, and the moon on the coin is a speck.
- Athena flying her owl (Met 50.11.1), for the Politics: handsome, but the juror's ticket shows citizenship itself.
- Bronze bull (Met 2021.40.59), for the History of Animals: no single passage ties it to the book; the hedgehog story does.
- Marble right hand and forearm, about 300 BC (Met 58.141), for Parts of Animals: nearer Aristotle's date, but the hand holding a book roll reads at once.
- Bronze youth (Met 08.258.10), for the Physics: the athlete's outline is cleaner.
- Fayum portraits of a young woman (Met 09.181.6) and the boy Eutyches (Met 18.9.2), for On Memory: the bearded man has more presence.
- Thunderbolt stater (Cleveland 1929.913.b), for the Meteorology: too worn.
- Terracotta head of an old woman (Met 10.210.94), for the Physiognomonica: the bronze head reads better small.
- Marble head of a philosopher (Met 26.269) and Rembrandt's Aristotle with a Bust of Homer (Met 61.198), for the home page: the first is not Aristotle, the second is not from his world.
- Mask (Met 13.231.3): the Met doubts its date.
- Octopus stirrup jar (Met 53.11.6) and the Paestan fish plate (Cleveland 1985.50): painted vases are the Greek Drama Reader's ground.
