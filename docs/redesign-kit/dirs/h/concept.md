# H · Plates

**The idea.** Each work gets one plate: an old print of a thing the work
names, cut hard, scaled up and printed in one blue ink beside plain type.
Each picture comes with the line of Aristotle it shows.

**Two sets.** A switch offers two sets in one layout. The later set, shown
first, is prints of 1700–1900: plates from encyclopedias, natural histories,
star books and catalogues of antiquities, with a few etchings. The early
set is the first one, mostly of the 1500s.

**Why it suits Aristotle.** His books are full of things you can draw: a
horse, a sea-fight, a mule, a crab that walks sideways, a seal in wax,
Atlas holding up the sky. The age of the encyclopedia drew such things
plainly, to explain them, and each picture points at a sentence in the text.

**Pages.**
- Home: Aristotle after the ancient bust; below, every work.
- A work: its plate fills most of the first screen, facing the title, with
  its line and source. Every work has a plate in the later set; in the early
  set seventeen get their Greek titles as plates instead.
- Reader: no picture. The ink marks chapter numbers, column starts and the
  open word.
- Search: the count in the ink. Lexicon: the headword as a plate.

**Type and colour.** Noto Serif Display, narrowed and heavy, for titles,
headings and large numerals (John, round seven: "Different font"); the switch
offers Bodoni Moda, EB Garamond, Instrument Serif, Piazzolla and round seven's
Archivo. Archivo for labels and controls, Piazzolla for reading, GFS Didot for
large Greek. The site's name sets its two words at one size and one width.
Black on cool white, one ultramarine ink. In dark mode line prints print
light; night scenes keep their night.

**Images and rights.** All public domain or CC0, credited under each plate.
The later set covers all 41 works and the home page, from the Wellcome
Collection, the Met, book scans at the Internet Archive, the Rijksmuseum, the
Cleveland Museum of Art and the Library of Congress. The paper is cleaned
away, so one file serves both themes.

**The risk.** A picture that needs its caption: young bees in the comb for
the Generation of Animals, Neptune's chariot for Gorgias.
