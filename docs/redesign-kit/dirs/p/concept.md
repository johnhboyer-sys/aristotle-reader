# P · Plato’s look

The Aristotle Reader in the Plato Reader’s current design, measured on plato.johnhboyer.com at 1440 and 390 and matched: Gentium Book Plus, Geologica capitals, the same bar, margins, rules, sizes and gaps. Each work page opens, as Plato’s do, on the work’s first forty-eight letters, twelve to a row, the last rows fading, with the Bekker line under them.

**Cloned:** the masthead (three even rows of eleven: ΑΡΙΣΤΟΤΕΛΗΣ in the accent, ΣΤΑΓΕΙΡΙΤΗΣ, ΤΑ ΣΩΙΖΟΜΕΝΑ with ΤΑ in grey), intro, search and “Begin with”; the works by division, Α to Ϛ; the work page’s block, titles, blurb, facts and buttons; the reader’s head bar and columns; search; the lexicon entry.

**Where John’s rules win:**
- On a phone, Greek and English stay side by side.
- Work pages list every chapter; books fold, closed at first.
- The Greek keeps Bekker’s line numbers in its left margin, the English its own beside it. The Greek runs as prose, as Plato’s does: Bywater’s lines are wider than the column.
- A Greek word opens Plato’s right sidebar, or a sheet from the bottom on a phone.
- The menu marks the current page. Translations appear once.
- On a phone every division stays open. The lexicon’s right column lists nearby words, not the works again.
- Search lists every work with hits down the left, as H’s does (John: "Put it on all the mock ups"); Plato’s shows eight and a “more” button.

**Palettes.** Green, my pick: Lyceum green on a grey with a breath of green. No sibling uses green, and it suits a school that taught walking in a grove. Red: a rubric red on neutral grey, for John’s liking of red, clear of Homer’s wine and Drama’s clay. Both have dark sets; text and accent pass 4.5:1.

**The risk:** it reads as Plato’s site in another colour. Aristotle’s longer name makes the masthead’s letters smaller than Plato’s. Three works show their titles, not their first words.
