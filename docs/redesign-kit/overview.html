The directions your notes kept in the running, side by side on the same six pages: B in Oswald, your pick of its heavier title faces, D as round four left it, H with a second set of plates and a new title face, and P, which puts Aristotle in the Plato Reader’s design. E, F and G are out; round six ↗ still shows them. The dashed switches in the bar show a direction’s other versions: B’s title faces, its photographs as printed and its logic works without blackboards, H’s 1500s plates and title faces, P in red. Keys: 1–4 pick a direction, v flips the first switch, f steps through B’s or H’s title faces, ← → step through the pages, t switches to dark, p switches between both widths, desktop and phone, n opens the designer’s notes.

After your notes

  • “E is too basic… F doesn’t work… G’s home, work, and the big quote on reader page are out.” E, F and G are not in this round.
  • “Either B or D or H.” B and D come back as rounds five and four left them, so all four sit on the same pages. One fix to both: Porphyry’s Isagoge now stands on their home pages, as the rule asks for every work.
  • “We might need to pick different plates… maybe not plates that are so renaissance.” H now opens on a second set of plates, from prints of about 1700–1870: encyclopedia and natural-history plates, star books and books of ancient objects, drawn to explain things. The 1500s set is one click away on the switch.
  • “If we doing plates, every work needs one.” The new set has a plate for every one of the 41 works and the home page, down to Movement of Animals, with Atlas holding up the heavens, and On Indivisible Lines, with a Roman folding foot-rule from Pompeii for its “unit of measurement”. The 1500s set still lacks 17.
  • “Different font.” H’s titles are now set in Noto Serif Display, a Didone, narrowed and heavy: the kind of type most of the later plates’ books were printed in, and kin to the Didot numerals already on the page. Narrowed, it still fills the column, as Archivo did. The one face now sets every title, heading and large numeral, and the logo; Archivo stays for labels and buttons. A new switch offers five others to compare: Bodoni Moda, EB Garamond, Instrument Serif, Piazzolla, the reading face set heavy, and Archivo as before; press f to step through them. This also mended a fault in the round-seven titles: their box cut off the tails of g, p and y, as in Categories. Every title now keeps its whole letters, in every face, at both widths.
  • “Reader and Aristotle should be same size.” In H’s logo, “Aristotle” and “Reader” now look the same size at both widths.
  • “Bigger, thicker font. Let’s try a few different fonts.” B’s titles now come in five heavier, narrower faces, with round five’s on the switch to compare: Sofia Sans Extra Condensed, Anton, Oswald, Bebas Neue and Big Shoulders. Narrower letters let each title grow: on a phone, Categories goes from 63 pixels to between 89 and 136. Each face follows round five’s rule, breaking a long word once at a syllable only where whole it would be under half its largest size. So Categories stays whole in the two narrowest and breaks CATE / GORIES in the other three.
  • “For B, I’m thinking Oswald for font.” B now opens in Oswald: the titles over the photographs on the home page and every work page, the division names and the reader’s BOOK I. The switch keeps the other faces to compare.
  • “For B, let’s find some photographs of logic being done on a chalkboard?” Three logic works now open on a blackboard. The Prior Analytics: Hippocrates’ lunes, labelled ΜΗΝΙΣΚΟΙ, the word Aristotle uses when the Analytics brings them in as an example (II.25). De Interpretatione: Greek past tenses, which it calls “not verbs, but tenses of a verb”. Both boards are from the Stedelijk Gymnasium in Utrecht, about a century ago. The Topics: an American classroom of about 1900, its board setting a lesson by induction, measuring circles to find how many diameters go into the circumference. A new switch, Logic works, brings back the photographs they replace, Le Gray’s fleet among them. The Categories, Posterior Analytics, Sophistical Refutations and Isagoge keep theirs: the boards I found don’t fit them as well as the horse, the rallying troops, the spirit photograph and the oak.
  • “Not loving the HERO text here…” B’s reader no longer opens on its first words in giant red capitals. Book I now opens on a plain head: the work’s name, then BOOK I in red Oswald, then the Greek title and the editions. See it.
  • “Smaller.” The count on the search page is smaller: from 150 to 88 pixels in B and from 168 to 96 in H; on a phone, from 96 to 60 and from 112 to 64. Your note sat on a spot the two pages share, so both changed.
  • “I like this feature. Put it on all the mock ups.” Every search page now lists the works with hits down the left, as H’s did: each work with its count, the open one marked, and the list stays in view as the hits scroll. On a phone it is a row to swipe. See B, D and P.
  • “Show the text/body paragraphs.” On a phone, D now shows the whole opening of each work in the Aldine edition: the woodcut headpiece, the title and the first lines of Greek, not the headpiece alone. See the home page.
  • “Having ‘B’ red makes no sense.” D’s book list is plain black now; a book turns red only when it is open or under the pointer.
  • “Where’s search?” and “The box around ‘Book I’ looks bad. The arrow should be enough.” The reader’s top bar now has search on a phone, and its Book and translation menus have no box, only the arrow. B and H had the same box and hid search the same way, so they have the same fix. See D’s reader.
  • “Make sure all the things in headers are aligned and look good. No uneven stuff.” In every top bar of the four directions, the words now sit on one baseline, whatever their size or face, and each icon centres on the capitals beside it. The menus and controls are spaced evenly: the same room either side of the Book menu, the view switch spaced like the rest, the theme icon as far from the menu as the links are from each other. On a phone, D’s logo now fits on one line, and P’s book and chapter menus get the same room as its icons.
  • “Maybe something else so we don’t have uneven rows?” P’s masthead ran twelve letters, then eight and eight. It now reads ΑΡΙΣΤΟΤΕΛΗΣ / ΣΤΑΓΕΙΡΙΤΗΣ / ΤΑ ΣΩΙΖΟΜΕΝΑ, “Aristotle the Stagirite: the surviving works”: three rows of exactly eleven letters, whole words only, so the grid is a full rectangle as Plato’s is. The iota is written beside its vowel, as on the work pages’ blocks, and the article is grey, as in Plato’s. See it.
  • “Or we just clone Plato’s current look with the opening text and a different palate.” That is P. Each work page opens on its first forty-eight letters, twelve to a row, the last rows fading, as Plato’s pages open on theirs. Two colours of Aristotle’s own: green, which no sibling site uses, and a red kept clear of Homer’s wine and Greek Drama’s clay.
  • “I like how the current Plato site breathes.” P keeps Plato’s spacing, measured on the live site at both widths: the same bar, margins, gaps, line heights and column widths. Where your rules add things Plato’s pages lack, such as the chapter lists, they get the same room.

Where P departs from Plato’s site

  • On a phone the reader keeps Greek and English side by side; Plato’s shows one.
  • A work page lists every chapter, under books that fold and start closed; Plato’s shows a grid of books.
  • The Greek runs as prose, as Plato’s does, with Bekker’s line numbers in its margin; the English keeps its own.
  • A Greek word opens a sidebar on the right, or a sheet from the bottom on a phone.
  • Translations appear once, and the menu marks Works on a work page too.
  • On a phone the home page’s divisions stay open, and the lexicon lists nearby words where Plato’s repeats the works.
  • Search lists every work with hits down the left, as the other three do; Plato’s shows eight and a “more” button.
  • The masthead reads ΑΡΙΣΤΟΤΕΛΗΣ ΣΤΑΓΕΙΡΙΤΗΣ ΤΑ ΣΩΙΖΟΜΕΝΑ, “Aristotle the Stagirite: the surviving works”: three rows of eleven letters, so the grid is as even as Plato’s three rows of eight.

What all four keep

  • Every work on the home page, and a page for each, listing every chapter under books that start closed.
  • Greek and English side by side in the reader, on a phone too, each with its own Bekker numbers; Greek, Both or English; two translations at once; notes; text size; contents; a jump to any Bekker line.
  • Click a Greek word and its parse opens beside the text, with the LSJ entry.
  • Search lists every work with hits beside the results, and the list stays in view as they scroll.
  • No pictures in the reader, search or lexicon. Light and dark, each designed.

Yours to decide

  • Which direction goes on: B, D, H or P.
  • For B: Oswald, or another face from the switch.
  • For H: the later plates or the 1500s, and the title face.
  • For P: green or red.

What the mockup leaves out

  • Only Book I of the Ethics opens in the reader.
  • Search shows one query, ἀρετή in any form; the lexicon shows ἀρετή.
  • Phrases, Support and the links to the other readers are placeholders.
  • Interface copy and captions are drafts.
