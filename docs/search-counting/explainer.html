Hits outnumber rows because one row counts every match in it. Row 6b16 is four hits: ἀρετὴ in the Greek, and “virtue” in Owen, Taylor, and Edghill.

So when the bar says “311 passages”, it is counting the grey labels, not the rows. That is why 311 and 83 pages seem not to fit: 311 is the smallest of the three numbers, and the pager is using the largest.

Today’s “and” matches by column, and a column can cross a chapter break. So it returns 311 columns spread over 238 chapters, and 10 of those chapters hold only one language. Nicomachean Ethics III 4 shows up for Rackham’s “the virtues are exercised” at 1113b, because the ἀρετή that matched sits in the same column but in chapter 3. Counting by chapter removes all 10.

How the pager cuts

The pager counts hits. It fills a page to about 40 hits, then starts the next, and it never splits a chapter. Page 1 stops at 39 hits. 2,952 hits at about 36 a page gives 83 pages. Because a row can hold 1 hit or 10, a page shows anywhere from 1 to 20 rows.

The fix you said yes to

“Page by passages, 40 a page” depends on which count is the passage.

A. Page by row

829 rows at 40 a page → about 21 pages, each the same length.
Every page looks alike, and the bar can say “829 passages”. The cost: rows exist only after the site loads the books, so it must load every book in the search before it can show page 1. For this search, that is every book of every work that mentions virtue.

B. Page by column

311 columns at 40 a page → about 8 pages, about 100 rows each.
Cheap: the pager knows the columns before any book loads. But pages get long, and they still vary in length, since one column can hold 1 row or 15. The bar should then say “311 columns”, not passages.

C. Keep the pager, fix the words

83 pages stay. The bar reads “2,952 hits in 311 columns”.
No code risk, and the numbers stop clashing. Pages stay short and uneven.
