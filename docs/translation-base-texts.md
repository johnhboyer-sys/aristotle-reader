## Aquinas, Commentary on De anima: Foster and Humphries (Yale / Routledge 1951)

- **Cleared to host: John, 2026-11-05.** Other translations still in copyright: John thinks them fair game for our purposes but has not said to use them.
- Latin translated: not confirmed. The only archive.org copy (aristotlesdeanim0000aris_z3n9, the 1965 printing) is lending-only. An unverified search summary says the paragraphs are numbered to match Pirotta's Marietti edition (Turin 1925). Isidore's HTML edition (Kenny) carries no translators' note.
- US registration: none. The Catalog of Copyright Entries, Books, 1951 and 1952 (archive.org catalogofcopyri351libr, catalogofcopyri361libr) has no entry under Foster, Humphries, Thomas Aquinas, Aristoteles, Moerbeke or "De anima". The same 1951 volume does list Conway's Aquinas on Ethics VII–IX (A53118), so the Aquinas heading was searched where entries exist.
- US renewal: no record found in the NYPL transcription of the 1978 and 1979 renewal registers (github.com/NYPL/cce-renewals). Stanford's renewal database turned away automated searches; a person can search it at https://exhibits.stanford.edu/copyrightrenewals.
- Both translators were English Dominicans and the book is cited with London and New Haven imprints of the same year (title page not seen). If Routledge published first and Yale's edition did not follow within 30 days, the 1996 URAA restoration would apply whether or not it was renewed. Which came first is not established.

## Aquinas, other Aristotle commentaries: Henry Regnery's Library of Living Catholic Thought

- **Commentary on the Metaphysics, John P. Rowan (Regnery, 1961, 2 vols).** **Ruled free to host, John, 2026-11-04.** **Text from isidore.co (Kenny's HTML), to be checked against the 1961 print: John, 2026-11-05. Not yet checked.** Registered: "© Henry Regnery Co.; 25Sep61; A538676" (Catalog of Copyright Entries, Books, Jul–Dec 1961, archive.org catalogofcopyrig3151libr). Renewal was due in 1989. None found in NYPL's 1988–1991 renewal files under Rowan, A538676, the title, Aquinas or Regnery; the same 1989 file does hold the renewals of two other 1961 books, *Catch-22* (RE419328) and *Stranger in a Strange Land* (RE428428), so the file covers that year. A US work, so URAA does not apply. Rowan's introduction: the translation "is based on the edition of Raymund M. Spiazzi, O.P.", fundamentally Cathala's, with Parma and Vivès consulted; commentary sections carry Cathala's numbers. Corpus Thomisticum's Metaphysics is "Textum Taurini 1950 editum", the same Spiazzi text. The book also translates Moerbeke's Latin of the Metaphysics. Source of the quotes: archive.org AquinasCommentaryOnTheMetaphysics (an unattributed upload of the text).

The site points to Corpus Thomisticum for the Latin and names no copyright or permission for any translation. Hosting there is not evidence of rights. It mirrors Joseph Kenny's old Dominican site, which the Dominicans took down over copyright (John, 2026-11-05). John is fine with grey; whether we may host a translation turns on that translation's own status, not on isidore's.

**aquinas.cc** (the Aquinas Institute's reader): Latin, English and Portuguese for every Aristotle commentary (Greek for most, Polish for the Ethics and Politics). No translator or Latin edition is named on the work pages or in the site's catalogue data; the streams are labelled only "Latina" and "English". For De anima:
- The English is Foster–Humphries revised. Aquinas n. 1: aquinas.cc "it is first of all necessary, as the Philosopher says in Parts of Animals, to consider separately what is common ... The reason for this procedure is that it saves frequent repetition"; Foster–Humphries "it is first of all necessary, as the Philosopher says in the De Animalibus [I, 5], to consider separately what is common to the class as a whole ... The reason for this procedure is that it saves frequent repetition." The English of Moerbeke is revised the same way.
- The Latin is not Corpus Thomisticum's. Aquinas n. 1 reads "Sicut Philosophus docet in XI De animalibus ... communia, [et seorsum] et postea" against CT's "Sicut docet philosophus in undecimo de animalibus ... communia et seorsum, et postea", and Moerbeke's text spells *endelichia*, *inpartibilis*, *quatinus*. The order, figures, brackets and spellings look like Gauthier's Leonine edition (1984). The page does not say so.
- Not checked: whose English the other commentaries carry (the browser stopped responding).
